the NNUE packages can now say where an evaluation comes from. Their `explain` command splits each position of a game into the five things a player names, so a commentary can say "no one is ahead in pieces, but White's king is the safer one". The 11 NNUE packages carry it (KP256 for Cloudflare Workers; KP256, HalfKP128, HalfKP256, HalfKP768 and NAGISA HalfKA1024 for browser threads and for Node.js). The `mate-*` packages are unchanged.

- **The five parts.** `explain moves 2g2f 3c3d` prints, for each position of the line, `parts`: **material** (what the pieces on the board and in hand are worth), **speed** (who is closer to mating whom, from checks and short mates), **king safety**, **piece activity** and **tempo** (the side to move). Positive favours Black. The five add up to a model's estimate of NAGISA_V4's evaluation, and each is scaled by how far the game has gone, so a quiet opening and an endgame race are weighed differently. It does not search and does not use the package's own network.
- **It needs two files you supply**; neither is bundled:
  - `HceProgressFile`: a 1.2 MB network that guesses how many moves the game has left from the board and the hands, printed as `moves_left`. It is separate from the progress that only NAGISA keeps, so the same parts work on every NNUE.
  - `HceExplainFile`: the model (about 1 MB) that holds the five parts. It records which progress network it was fitted against, and `explain` prints no parts when the two files do not match.
  - Without them `explain` prints what it printed before: the linear breakdown. `HceSpeed` (off by default) also prints the eight numbers behind the speed part.
- **How close it is to NAGISA_V4**, on 1,235,058 positions that were not used to fit it (it was fitted on 42,919,014, scored after a quiescence search):
  - The miss is 416 root-mean-square, against 895 for always saying 0. It is within 500 on 85% of positions and off by 1000 or more on 3.2%. It puts the wrong side ahead by 200 or more, where NAGISA_V4 has one side ahead by 500 or more, on 0.9% of positions.
  - It is better early than late. By NAGISA's own progress, split in thirds (the games' average moves left are 54, 31 and 18), the miss is 183, 373 and 630. The endgame, where mates and attacks decide, is where it says least.
  - Take the positions where NAGISA_V4 has one side ahead by 500 or more. In 66.6% of them the pieces agree by 300 or more, and the five parts added up are on NAGISA_V4's side in 98.5%. In 8.1% the pieces are within 90 of even, and the sum is on NAGISA_V4's side in 90.4%. In 7.4% the pieces are against it by 300 or more; there the sum is still on its side in 70.8%, and the largest part pointing NAGISA_V4's way is king safety in 60% of them. The rest are in between.
  - Material is the piece-value table, with what the 17 piece counts explain in the other parts moved into it. In internal units a pawn is 38, lance 84, knight 135, silver 249, gold 290, bishop 350, rook 393, horse 506 and dragon 616; in hand a gold is 356 and a rook 448.
- **How it was checked.** Each piece was compared with the Python that trained it, on thousands of held-out positions: moves left on 3,000, the board facts on 3,000, the eight speed numbers on 4,000, and the five parts on 3,000, equal to within the rounding of an integer. On one SFNN build the `bench` node count is the same with the command in and out (272,452). All 11 packages were built in CI, and `explain` was run with both files in the KP256 Node.js package; the other packages were built, not run.
- **Building it yourself.** `make YANEURAOU_EDITION=<nnue edition> HCE_EXPLAIN=ON`. It is off by default, and with it off the build is the same as before. The code is in `source/eval/hce/`.
