the NNUE packages now carry an `explain` command that says, for a position and a line of play, where an evaluation comes from. It is built into every NNUE package (KP256, HalfKP128, HalfKP256, HalfKP768 and the SFNN NAGISA network, in the browser, Node.js and Cloudflare Workers builds). The `mate-*` packages are untouched, and so are the search, the evaluation and the `bench` node counts of every other package: they are what they were with the command in and out.

- **`explain` splits a position into the five things a player names.** For each position along a line it prints `parts`: **material** (what the pieces on the board and in hand are worth), **speed** (who is closer to mating whom, from checks and short mates), **king safety**, **piece activity**, and **tempo** (the side to move). The five add up to a model's estimate of a strong NNUE's evaluation, and each is scaled by how far the game has gone, so a quiet opening and a race in the endgame are weighed differently.
  - It describes a position the way a commentator would; it does not search, and it does not call the NNUE of the package. It was fitted to NAGISA_V4's scores on 42.9 million positions after a quiescence search.
  - How well it does, on 1.2 million positions it was not fitted on: the estimate is within 500 of NAGISA_V4's score on 85% of positions (the root-mean-square miss is 417 against 895 for a constant 0), and it puts a side clearly ahead when NAGISA_V4 does, with the wrong sign by 200 or more on 0.9% of all positions. Where it has least to say is the endgame, where mates and attacks decide: its miss is 631 in the last third of progress against 183 in the first.
  - A position where the pieces say one side is ahead by 300 or more and NAGISA_V4 puts the other side ahead by 500 or more is the one a commentator needs most. There the five parts, added up, still agree with NAGISA_V4 on 71% of positions, and king safety is the leading reason in 60% of them.
- **It needs two files you supply**, neither bundled in the package:
  - `HceProgressFile`: a 1.2 MB network that guesses how many moves a game has left from the board and the hands (`moves_left` in the output). It stands in for the progress that only NAGISA keeps, so the same parts work on every NNUE.
  - `HceExplainFile`: the model (about 1 MB) that holds the five parts. It records which progress network it was fitted against and is used only with that one; `explain` prints no parts if the two do not match.
  - Without them `explain` prints what it printed before: the linear breakdown. `HceSpeed` (default off) adds the eight speed columns to every node, which search for mates and cost more.
- **The pieces behind it** (all in `source/eval/hce/`): the model reader, the per-square facts that king safety and piece activity read, the speed columns, a small network evaluator and the moves-left network. Each was compared with the Python that trained it, on thousands of held-out positions, and printed the same numbers to within the rounding of an integer.
- **Building it yourself**: `make YANEURAOU_EDITION=<nnue edition> HCE_EXPLAIN=ON`. It is off by default, and with it off the build is the same as before.
